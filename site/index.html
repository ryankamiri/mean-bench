Why Mean Bench?

Open a coding leaderboard and one model wins. Switch to accounting, then web research, and you’ve got three champions and a small tab problem.

So I built Mean Bench to average it out. It uses the results each model has across 25 benchmarks and shows how many went into its score.

    Methodology

    How scores, coverage, and cost are calculated for this snapshot.

    Authored by Ryan Amiri

    Model and benchmark eligibility

    The snapshot covers June 18 through November 18, 2026, inclusive. Selected models are excluded from the public ranking. A benchmark needs scores for at least 5 displayed models. Ranked models have at least one result on the resulting suite.

    Average score and coverage

    Each benchmark contributes one equally weighted score on a 0–100 scale. The mean divides the sum by the number of scored benchmarks. Coverage shows that count as n/25. Missing results are omitted from the mean.

    Score selection and scale

    Selection prefers the configured board and source, then the highest reported reasoning effort. Tied results use the observation nearest the median. Percentage scores keep their published values. GDPval and Arena ratings use expected scores against fixed reference ratings. AA-Briefcase uses Artificial Analysis’s normalization, and AA-Omniscience is linearly rescaled.

    Cost and comparability

    Cost averages the published USD per task or trial among a model’s selected scores with cost data. The observed Pareto frontier connects models with the highest score at their cost or less, with ties going to the cheaper model. Models can have different benchmark mixes, harnesses, effort levels, and cost coverage.
